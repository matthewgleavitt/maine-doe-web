The Maine Department of Education has received $1,021,690,359 in federal emergency relief funding to improve student learning, address unfinished learning, support students' mental health, and combat pandemic-related challenges. The three federal emergency relief acts are:

Update — April 8, 2025: The U.S. Department of Education has discontinued annual performance reporting for Elementary and Secondary School Emergency Relief (ESSER) funds. SAUs are no longer required to submit ESSER performance reports to the Maine Department of Education. Contact Shelly.Chassejohndro@maine.gov with questions.

  • Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security (CARES) Act — enacted March 27, 2020 to address the impact that COVID-19 has had, and continues to have, on elementary and secondary schools.
  • Coronavirus Response and Relief Supplemental Appropriations (CRRSA) Act — enacted December 27, 2020 to prevent, prepare for, and respond to COVID-19 related to addressing learning loss, preparing schools for reopening, and testing, repairing, and upgrading projects to improve air quality in school buildings.
  • American Rescue Plan (ARP) Act — enacted March 11, 2021 to safely reopen and sustain the safe operation of schools and address the impact of the coronavirus pandemic.

Any use of ESSER funds must be "to prevent, prepare for, and respond to" the COVID-19 pandemic while being necessary and reasonable for the performance of the ESSER award. Transferring funds to a rainy-day fund, bridging a budget shortfall unrelated to the pandemic, and providing matching funds for other federal programs are not allowable uses.
