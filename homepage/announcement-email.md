Hi everyone,

The Maine Department of Education website has a new homepage, and our content
pages are moving to a new, consistent design. Both are live now at
[maine.gov/doe](https://www.maine.gov/doe) — no action is needed from anyone,
but here's what changed and why.

## The homepage

The old homepage asked visitors to hunt. The new one leads with what people
actually come for.

- **A featured story area** at the top that rotates through current
  announcements, resources, conferences, professional learning, and
  recognitions — each labelled so you can see at a glance what kind of thing
  it is. It pauses when you touch or hover it, so nothing moves out from under
  you while you're reading.
- **The newsroom** in a clean set of cards, all the same size regardless of the
  image that comes with the story.
- **A calendar** that reads properly on a phone.
- **"Follow Maine DOE"** gathered in one place at the foot of the page, so our
  social channels and the newsletter are findable from anywhere.
- **A rebuilt mobile experience.** The menu, search, and section links were the
  weakest part of the old site on a phone, which is how most people reach us.

## The content pages

Our interior pages were built over many years by many people, and it showed.
The same kind of information appeared in half a dozen different treatments
depending on which page you landed on. The new design gives them one shared set
of components.

- **A consistent page header.** Every page gets the same treatment at the top,
  with room for a short description where one is useful — and it's built so that
  pages with a description and pages without still look like they belong
  together.
- **Optional imagery.** A page can carry a photograph in its header if it has
  one worth showing. Pages without one aren't the poor relation — they read as
  the same design, just quieter.
- **A section navigator on longer pages**, so people can jump straight to the
  part they need instead of hunting through several screens for it.
- **Consistent branding across pages** — the same corners, spacing, colours, and
  type throughout, so cards, tables, expandable sections, and contact blocks
  look the same wherever you meet them. Tables in particular read at full size
  now, with room to breathe.
- **Links to documents show their format** — a small label saying PDF, Word, or
  YouTube — so you know what you're about to open.
- **Shortcuts back to the top and straight to the contact details**, because on
  a long page those are the two things people most often want and least often
  find.

## Why it looks the way it does

Two things drove most of the decisions.

**Long pages are hard to read, and we have a lot of them.** Some of our guidance
pages run to several thousand words. The answer isn't shorter content — the
content is there because people need it — it's giving the eye somewhere to land,
and a way to skip ahead.

**Accessibility is a floor, not a finish.** Every text and background
combination on the pages we tested meets the WCAG 2.1 AA contrast standard at
both desktop and narrow widths. Headings follow a proper order so screen readers
announce the page structure correctly, links describe where they go rather than
saying "click here," and controls are large enough to hit comfortably.

## What this means if you maintain pages

Your content stays editable the way it always has been — the design sits on top
of the page, not inside your text. Some of the newer pieces, like the section
navigator, involve a bit of setup behind the scenes, and that's my job rather
than yours. Nothing is broken and nothing needs doing today.

**We'll be holding office hours soon** to walk through the Communications Portal
website updates and answer questions about these changes — details to follow.
If you'd rather not wait, or you spot something that looks wrong, send it my way
any time.
